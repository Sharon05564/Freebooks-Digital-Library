YOUR DATA, IN PLAIN WORDS

Privacy at FreeBooks.

Last updated November 5, 2026.

Your account and bookshelf

If you create an account with email, FreeBooks stores your username, email address, and a hashed password. If you use Google, FreeBooks receives and stores your Google account ID, name, verified email address, and profile photo when provided. We do not receive your Google password.

FreeBooks stores the books you save, including their titles, authors, cover images, and reading links, so you can return to your bookshelf.

Recommendations

If you are signed in, FreeBooks stores the reading interests you choose and your Interested or Not for me responses. A content-based recommendation model uses these choices and your saved books to suggest related classics. You can change your genres, undo feedback, or clear your recommendation preferences. Guest genre choices stay in your browser. A few saved book identifiers may be looked up through Google Books to fetch book descriptions. Your name and email are not sent to an AI model provider.

Signing in

Your browser stores a login token and basic account details in local storage. Google sign-in also uses a short-lived, essential session cookie to validate the sign-in request. Logging out removes the login token and account details from that browser.

Services used by the app

FreeBooks runs on Render and stores account and bookshelf data in Neon Postgres. Book searches are sent to Google Books. Reading a book or opening an external book link takes you to a third-party service with its own privacy practices.

If the contact form’s email delivery is enabled, your name, email, subject, and message are sent through Resend to the site operator. Hosting providers may process technical request logs to run and troubleshoot the app.

Your choices

You can explore books without an account, remove books from your bookshelf, update your account name and email, and log out at any time. For account deletion or other privacy requests, use the FreeBooks support email shown on Google’s sign-in consent screen. Removing Google access in your Google account settings does not automatically delete your FreeBooks account or bookshelf.

Google permissions

FreeBooks requests only your basic Google profile and email for authentication. It does not request access to Gmail, Drive, contacts, or private Google Books shelves.
